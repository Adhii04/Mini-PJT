7. Model Limitations

  • Survival analysis assumes non-informative censoring — employees who are still active are assumed to resign eventually.
  • Cox PH assumes proportional hazards — the effect of each feature is constant over time. This may not hold for all features.
  • The model was trained on historical data and may not capture future organizational changes.
  • Predictions are probabilistic estimates, not deterministic outcomes.
  • Small sample sizes or low event rates may reduce model reliability.
  • SHAP explanations are approximations using KernelExplainer due to the non-standard RSF model interface.

8. Business Interpretation

The C-index measures how well the model ranks employees by resignation risk. A C-index of 0.5 = random guessing, 1.0 = perfect ranking. The Integrated Brier Score measures calibration accuracy — lower is better. The hazard ratios indicate the multiplicative effect of each feature on the resignation hazard. A hazard ratio > 1 means that feature increases risk; < 1 means it decreases risk. Risk categories are computed from the percentile distribution of predicted resignation timelines, making them adaptive to the specific dataset rather than hardcoded.
